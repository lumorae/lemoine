I couldn't design anything for High Spirits until I drove out to the desert and watched the flutes get made.

For a while I thought that was me being precious about process. Now I think it was the actual job.

High Spirits has been making flutes by hand for decades. When they asked me to rebuild the brand and the website, the easy move would have been to open the laptop and start. Warm colors, nice type, a few photos of wood. Done in a week.

But I knew I would be faking it. You cannot translate a feeling you have not actually felt yet.

So I drove from San Diego to Patagonia, Arizona, and stood in the room where it happens. Odell picks up a piece of wood and already seems to know the note living inside it. Raw wood at one end of the bench, something that sings at the other.

I left with a full notebook and one sentence. A company that has made every instrument by hand for this long should have a website built with that same patience.

That sentence decided everything. Hundreds of small illustrations instead of stock photos. A typeface I hand lettered from scratch. Pages that ask you to slow down instead of scroll past.

I think a lot of branding goes wrong right here. People try to design the feeling before they have felt it, so the work ends up looking like the category instead of the company.

The visuals should carry the level of care already inside the work.

Most of the time that means going to where the work is made, and listening before you make anything at all.

It went live this week. If you want to hear what I mean, turn your sound on at highspirits.com.
