How I work

Written for SOMA Laboratory · July 2026

You asked how I approach a project. This is the honest version — the same shape every time, whether it is an instrument maker in Russia or a flute workshop in Arizona.

It is worth saying first what I would not do here. SOMA already has a world: a philosophy, a vocabulary, a way of thinking about sound that almost nobody else is doing. I would not be inventing one. The work would be making sure that world survives the translation to a screen — that someone who finds you through a search result arrives somewhere as considered as the instrument they were reading about.

Who is writing this

I spent fifteen years as an architect before starting this studio. That is the whole explanation for how I work: a building cannot be decorated into coherence, it has to be structured into it, and a brand is no different. You find the one organising idea, and then every later decision either serves it or gets cut.

I run Lemoine as a small studio and take few projects at a time, on purpose. You would be working with me — not a team you meet once at the start. I am also a working artist, which is probably why I keep ending up alongside people who make physical things: flutes, chocolate, wine, instruments. I understand the particular problem of an object that is wonderful in the hand and flattened into a photograph on a website.

The process, in five stages

01

Listening, before anything is designed

I read everything a company has already written about itself, and I talk to the people who built it. Not a survey — a conversation. The stage ends when I can explain your company back to you in your own words and you recognise it. Nothing gets drawn before that.

02

One foundational idea

Every project I take resolves into a single idea that everything afterwards has to answer to. It is the slowest part and the one that matters most. Once it exists, decisions stop being matters of taste: a choice either serves the idea or it does not, and that is a question we can both answer without arguing about colour.

03

Identity — made by hand, built to move

Marks, type, colour, motion. I draw by hand and then build in code, so the identity is not a static file you receive and then struggle to apply. It arrives as a working system, with the reasoning attached, so your team can keep using it correctly after I am gone.

04

The site, designed and built as one thing

I design the website and I build it myself. There is no handoff, so nothing is lost in it — no compromise where the developer says the animation is not possible, no drift between the drawing and the thing that ships. It also means the site is structured to grow: new instruments, new writing, new languages, without a rebuild.

05

Staying past launch

You work with me directly, from the first conversation to launch and after it. Part of what I am there to do is push back when a decision weakens the idea — including my own decisions. A brand is only worth what it is worth in five years.

Where I would start with SOMA

Not proposals — questions. These would be the first three, whenever the time is right.

  1. Which part of SOMA's world do you feel the website currently fails to carry?
  2. Who is the person you most want to reach and are not reaching yet — and what stops them?
  3. What must never change, no matter who redesigns it?
