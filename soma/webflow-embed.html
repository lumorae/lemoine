how i work.

Written for SOMA Laboratory · July 2026

You asked how I approach a project, so I made this page to give you a proper answer.

SOMA has a lot to hold together. A growing family of instruments. A philosophy that reaches far beyond the products themselves. An international team. Direct sales and dealers around the world. Music releases, interviews, art, videos, community projects, and years of ideas surrounding the work.

My first job would be to understand how all of it works today, and what the next version of SOMA needs to make clearer, easier, and stronger.

Who's writing this

I spent fifteen years working as an architect before starting Lemoine. That experience still shapes almost everything I do. Architecture teaches you to understand the full context, find the idea that will guide the work, coordinate a lot of moving parts, and care as much about the smallest detail as the overall experience. That's how I approach brands and websites now.

I'm also an artist and musician. My work moves across brand strategy, identity, web, packaging, and physical spaces. That range lets me understand a company from several angles and stay involved from the first conversation through the finished work.

For SOMA that feels especially useful. The instruments, interfaces, packaging, writing, photography, videos, music, and website all contribute to how someone understands the company. I would look at the relationships between those things and help them feel more connected.

The process

01

we start by getting connected.

Every project begins with an in-depth strategy workshop with the people closest to the work. In a room when that's possible, and just as well over video or in writing when it isn't. I prepare twenty to twenty-five questions written specifically for the company. They're thoughtful, unexpected, and occasionally a little strange, and they're designed to get past rehearsed answers to what people actually believe, care about, want to protect, and hope to build next.

In about an hour we usually uncover a lot. The purpose of the company, the people it serves, the personality behind it, where it's heading, and the experience it should create. Everyone hears how the rest of the team sees it. Agreement gets clearer, and differences come into the open while they're still useful.

This is where alignment begins, and it's by far my favorite part of the process.

02

i build the strategy.

After the workshop I go deep: the company, audience, market, competitors, current brand, and every part of the experience that matters to the project. With SOMA that would mean time with the instruments and manuals, the philosophy, the product categories, the interviews, the music label, the art and literature projects, the dealer network, the regional teams, and the way people currently move between all of it.

Then I start writing. The strategy defines the purpose, positioning, audience, values, voice, personality, and central idea, and turns everything we discussed into something the team can make decisions with. I present it live so we can talk it through. By the end you should recognize SOMA in it and feel clear about the direction.

03

i develop the identity.

Once the strategy is aligned I step away from the screen and start sketching: shapes, symbols, typography, composition, color, image, whatever begins to emerge from the idea. I let this part stay loose for a while. Early work needs room to go somewhere unexpected before it gets refined.

From there I develop two complete visual directions. Both come from the same strategy, and each shows how the brand works in the places people actually meet it: the website, product presentation, packaging, photography, video, editorial, social, launches. We choose the strongest and refine it through a focused round of feedback.

For SOMA I'd start by understanding which parts of the existing identity are essential, and where the system needs more clarity, consistency, or room to grow. When the direction is complete I write the guidelines and organize the assets so everyone working with the brand knows how to use them.

04

i design the website.

I approach websites much the way I approached buildings. I begin with structure: where people enter, what they need to understand first, where they go next, how the parts relate.

SOMA's site has to serve several kinds of people at once. Someone discovering you through the LYRA-8. An experienced musician comparing synths. A customer hunting for a manual or a dealer. An artist deep in the interviews and music. A team member publishing a new product, video, project, or translation. The structure has to work for all of them without flattening what makes SOMA interesting, and even more so, what makes it flourish into a work of art, just like the instruments you make yourself.

I map the site and design the key pages as working prototypes, so we review hierarchy, navigation, content, motion, and the overall experience before the whole thing is built. Then I carry the approved direction through the remaining pages and responsive layouts. Depending on the technical needs I build it myself or work directly with a specialist developer while continuing to lead the design.

You work with me throughout. The person in the early strategy conversations is the same person making the decisions later. After launch I train the team, hand over the assets and guidelines, and stay close through the first week to make sure everything works as intended.

Where my range becomes useful

High Spirits Flutes

The closest single project I've made to this. They've been making Native American flutes in Arizona for nearly forty years. I visited the workshop, learned how the instruments are made, studied how people choose between hundreds of products, and worked closely with the team to understand what the company meant to the people who play its flutes.

I led the strategy, identity, illustration, custom typography, product organization, e-commerce experience, and the website design and build. The project moved between craftsmanship, sound, culture, education, product data, storytelling, commerce, and the practical needs of the people running the company. Every part came from the same foundation we created together.

Strategy · Identity · Custom typography · Illustration · E-commerce

High Spirits is the closest reference. The broader fit comes from the rest of the practice: packaging systems spanning more than ninety products, books, websites, custom type, illustration, environmental graphics, and large-scale murals, plus fifteen years designing buildings before any of it.

I can zoom out far enough to understand a complicated company, and all the way into the detail someone touches, reads, hears, or remembers. I also know when a project needs another specialist, and when it does, they arrive to a clear foundation and a shared direction.

Where I'd start with SOMA

  • If SOMA were a person, how would you describe them to a close friend? What do they sound like, and how do they make you feel after hanging out with them?
  • What has SOMA outgrown?
  • Which parts of the current brand feel essential to preserve?
  • How should the instruments, philosophy, music, interviews, projects, community, and buying experience relate to one another?
  • What does your team need the website to make easier?
