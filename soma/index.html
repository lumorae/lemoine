Lemoine — Brand & Web

how i work.

Written for SOMA Laboratory · July 2026

You asked how I approach a project. Here's the honest answer. It's the same shape every time, whether it's an instrument maker in Russia or a flute workshop in Arizona.

First, what I wouldn't do. SOMA already has a world — a philosophy, a vocabulary, a way of thinking about sound that nobody else is doing. I wouldn't be inventing one. My job would be making sure that world survives the trip to a screen, so someone who finds you through a search result lands somewhere as considered as the instrument they were reading about.

Who's writing this

I spent fifteen years as an architect before I started this studio, and that explains most of how I work. You can't decorate a building into coherence. You have to structure it in. A brand is no different — find the one idea holding it up, and everything after that either serves it or gets cut.

I run Lemoine as a small studio and take a few projects at a time, on purpose. You'd be working with me, start to finish. I'm also a working artist, which is probably why I keep ending up next to people who make physical things — flutes, chocolate, wine, instruments. I know the particular problem of an object that's wonderful in your hands and flat in a photograph.

The process

01

I listen first

I read everything you've already written about yourselves, and I talk to the people who built the place. Not a survey — a conversation. This part's done when I can explain your company back to you in your own words and you recognize it. Nothing gets drawn before then.

02

One idea holds it up

Every project I take comes down to a single idea that everything else answers to. It's the slowest part and it's the one that matters. Once it's there, decisions stop being about taste — a choice either serves the idea or it doesn't, and we can both see which.

03

Identity, drawn by hand

Marks, type, color, motion. I draw by hand and then build in code, so what you get isn't a folder of files you have to work out how to use. It's a working system with the reasoning attached, so your team keeps it right after I'm gone.

04

The site is designed and built by one person

Me. There's no handoff, so nothing gets lost in one — no "the developer says that animation isn't possible," no drift between the drawing and the thing that ships. It also means the site is built to grow: new instruments, new writing, new languages, without starting over.

05

I stay past launch

Part of what I'm there for is to push back when a decision weakens the idea — mine included. A brand is only worth what it's worth in five years.

Strategy, identity, web, and packaging, usually together, because the idea leaks when it passes between people. Packaging is worth naming for you specifically: you ship physical instruments, and the box is the first thing someone touches after they've paid.

The closest thing I've made to this

High Spirits Flutes

A workshop in Arizona that's been making flutes by hand for decades. An instrument maker with a real philosophy and a website that wasn't carrying any of it. I did the strategy, the identity, and an online store built to sell handmade instruments to people who'll never hold one before they buy. That's your problem too.

Where I'd start with SOMA

Questions, not proposals. These would be my first three, whenever the time is right.

  1. Which part of SOMA's world does the website not carry yet?
  2. Who do you most want to reach and aren't reaching — and what's stopping them?
